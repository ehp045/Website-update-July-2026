The Sentence I Rewrote Nine Times

June 2, 2026 · 4 min read

There's a sentence in an essay I published last year that I rewrote nine separate times before it was right. I want to walk through what each draft got wrong, because the mistakes are more instructive than the fix.

The first draft was accurate and dead on arrival: it told the reader what happened without making them feel anything about it. The next several drafts overcorrected — I kept adding detail until the sentence collapsed under its own weight. By draft six I'd cut so much that I'd lost the one detail that mattered.

What the ninth draft did differently

The ninth draft worked because I finally stopped trying to fix the sentence and asked what job it was supposed to do in the paragraph. Once I knew the job, the right version took about ninety seconds to write.

That's the part I try to remember whenever I'm stuck: the fix is rarely a better word. It's usually a clearer idea of what the sentence is for.
